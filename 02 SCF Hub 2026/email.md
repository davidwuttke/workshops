**Subject: SCF Hub 2026 – proposed questions and discussion**

Dear all,

Thank you very much for joining our SCF Hub on 1 December, 15:00–16:00 CET. I am delighted to have you with us.

**1. Structure**

Brief welcome → opening questions (**two minutes each**) → panel discussion (30 minutes) → wrap-up.

**2. Opening questions, in speaking order**

- **Frank:** What has made PUMA's approach successful, and what can others learn about using financing incentives to support suppliers' climate progress?
- **Tom:** From your experience at Orbian, can SCF help suppliers invest in lower-emission operations without an explicit climate reward? What works in practice?
- **Lena:** How much demand do CRX clients express for dedicated climate-finance instruments, and what would make these valuable to them?
- **Karel:** Building on Calculum's work, how could AI support climate-related measurement and automatically identify opportunities where financing could unlock improvements?
- **Friedemann:** What has Siemens done particularly well in scaling SCF, and how could those lessons help suppliers finance lower-emission or climate-resilient operations?
- **Thomas Voland:** How regularly does climate arise in client conversations, and are companies comfortable addressing it openly when political support differs across countries?
- **Peter:** Looking across markets, where do you see the most promising progress in climate finance, and what should companies take encouragement from?

**3. Three theses for discussion**

The panel will be slightly different this time: instead of preparing further questions for each of you, I would love us to explore arguments for and against these three theses, building on each other's perspectives:

1. For many suppliers, reliable access to liquidity matters more than a green pricing bonus.
2. To scale climate-linked SCF, we should reward measurable progress before asking every supplier for perfect data.
3. The strongest climate-finance programmes will be those that make business sense even when political support is weak.

Please feel very welcome to share feedback; I would be happy to adjust the questions or theses together.

With many thanks and best wishes,
David
