## Karte 2: Assistance and learning (14:17)

Etwa drei Minuten für die beiden Studien, ihre Einordnung und die Übergabe. Die AR-Forschung dient als Ausgangspunkt für die Fragen an Physical AI.

**Introduce the research**

> To frame that question, let me share two studies of augmented reality in manufacturing and repair operations.

**Study 1: AR guidance — the GPS analogy**

- Follow every turn; reach the destination. But have you learned the route?
- Similar pattern in our AR study: assistance can outpace learning.
- Keep the distinction clear: completing today's task and building expertise for tomorrow.

> Doing the task is not the same as learning it.

**Study 2: remote support**

- Next application: AR for remote support.
- Two learning channels: partner exposure and team familiarity.
- In our study, the learning gains from these two channels did not materialise under AR.

**Management takeaway**

- Both applications look attractive on paper, but require management.
- Before implementation at scale, understand how the technology interacts with our greatest asset: the workforce.
- Bring that question into the physical AI discussion: what changes for people's skills and opportunities to learn?

**Transition to the panel, by 14:20**

> Three short impulses, then two pairs of opposing theses.

- Ahr, Engel, Grunwald; Wibrow joins the discussion.
- Pair 1: replacing skilled workers or capturing the boomers' knowledge?
- Pair 2: a shortcut to expertise for Gen Z, or a substitute for learning?
- Introduce Helmut Ahr using card 3.

## Karte 7: Paar 1, The boomers. What is industrial AI for? (14:32, Folie 1/2)

**A:** If industrial AI helps, it is because we can finally afford fewer expensive skilled workers.

**B:** If industrial AI helps, it is because it catches the boomers' knowledge before it retires.

**So läuft es:** Hands in the room: A or B? Dann Ahr, Engel, Grunwald, Wibrow. A or B, one reason, 45 seconds. No "both are right".

**Eine Frage pro Person**

- **Ahr:** One humanoid for 2.5 workers. But your robots learn from today's skilled workers. So: replace them, or learn from them before they retire?
- **Engel:** The Cutting Assistant needs no cutting experience. Is that A or B: fewer experienced operators, or keeping what they know?
- **Grunwald:** 30 percent leaner, 1,000 AI experts in Berlin. Fewer expensive engineers, or knowledge brought back in-house?
- **Wibrow:** Autonomous trucks: fewer drivers, or preserving their know-how? Which experienced judgement is hardest to capture?

**Wenn Zeit bleibt**

- An Ahr: The boomers are gone by 2037, your robots reach 50 percent after 2040. Who runs the lines in between?
- An Engel: When a customer's best operator trains your system, whose knowledge is it afterwards?
- An Grunwald: A humanoid that gets its skills as a software update from the provider: is that the supplier mistake again, on the shop floor?

## Karte 8: Paar 2, Gen Z. What does industrial AI do to them? (14:38, Folie 2/2)

**A:** For Gen Z, industrial AI is a shortcut to expertise. They start where the boomers ended.

**B:** For Gen Z, industrial AI is a substitute for expertise. They never have to learn what the machine already knows and will never become as much experts as boomers are.

**So läuft es:** Hands in the room: A or B? Dann Engel, Grunwald, Ahr, Wibrow. 45 seconds each.

**Eine Frage pro Person**

- **Engel:** A beginner cuts good parts on day one. Two years later: an expert, or a beginner with a very good assistant?
- **Grunwald:** If AI writes the simple code, who becomes your next architect?
- **Ahr:** Your roadmap reads like an apprenticeship: the robot takes the simple tasks first. Where does someone who starts in 2030 learn to handle a disruption?
- **Wibrow:** When a truck handles routine moves, how does a beginner learn to deal with exceptions?

**Wenn Zeit bleibt**

- An Grunwald: CARIAD engineers were trained on site at XPeng and now write that code themselves. A shortcut, or simply learning, from people?
- An Ahr: After 2040 the robot handles the faults too. What is the other 50 percent, and who is able to do it?
