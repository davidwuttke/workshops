## Karte 4: Paar 1, The boomers. What is industrial AI for? (14:32, Folie 1/2)

**A:** If industrial AI helps, it is because we can finally afford fewer expensive skilled workers.

**B:** If industrial AI helps, it is because it catches the boomers' knowledge before it retires.

**So läuft es:** Hands in the room: A or B? Dann Ahr, Engel, Grunwald. A or B, one reason, one minute. No "both are right".

**Eine Frage pro Person**

- **Ahr:** One humanoid for 2.5 workers. But your robots learn from today's skilled workers. So: replace them, or learn from them before they retire?
- **Engel:** The Cutting Assistant needs no cutting experience. Is that A or B: fewer experienced operators, or keeping what they know?
- **Grunwald:** 30 percent leaner, 1,000 AI experts in Berlin. Fewer expensive engineers, or knowledge brought back in-house?

**Wenn Zeit bleibt**

- An Ahr: The boomers are gone by 2037, your robots reach 50 percent after 2040. Who runs the lines in between?
- An Engel: When a customer's best operator trains your system, whose knowledge is it afterwards?
- An Grunwald: A humanoid that gets its skills as a software update from the provider: is that the supplier mistake again, on the shop floor?

## Karte 5: Paar 2, Gen Z. What does industrial AI do to them? (14:38, Folie 2/2)

**A:** For Gen Z, industrial AI is a shortcut to expertise. They start where the boomers ended.

**B:** For Gen Z, industrial AI is a substitute for expertise. They never have to learn what the machine already knows and will never become as much experts as boomers are.

**So läuft es:** Hands in the room: A or B? Dann Engel, Grunwald, Ahr.

**Eine Frage pro Person**

- **Engel:** A beginner cuts good parts on day one. Two years later: an expert, or a beginner with a very good assistant?
- **Grunwald:** If AI writes the simple code, who becomes your next architect?
- **Ahr:** Your roadmap reads like an apprenticeship: the robot takes the simple tasks first. Where does someone who starts in 2030 learn to handle a disruption?

**Wenn Zeit bleibt**

- An Grunwald: CARIAD engineers were trained on site at XPeng and now write that code themselves. A shortcut, or simply learning, from people?
- An Ahr: After 2040 the robot handles the faults too. What is the other 50 percent, and who is able to do it?

**Schluss, 14:44**

> Both A options show up in this year's numbers. Both B options show up in ten years. That is why companies end up with fewer people and no new experts without ever deciding to.

Variante mit Ahrs Zahlen, falls niemand eine Zahl für B genannt hat: We heard a number for A today: one robot for 2.5 workers, at ten euros an hour. Nobody gave us a number for B. That is why A happens by default.
